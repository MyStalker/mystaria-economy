# Three National Economic Profiles — Working Framework

**Status:** Author-described qualitative direction, November 21, 2026. No wages, prices, population ratios or laws are quantified or approved here.

## Imperium — Broad free-citizen abundance, highly unequal rights
- Massive exploitation through slavery supports the prosperity of a technologically advanced society.
- Freed people enjoy an almost comically modern quality of life, with even poor free people accessing substantial luxuries.
- **Interpretive caution:** This is the standard of living among free people, not proof that enslaved people enjoy equivalent resources or rights.
- Supporting mechanisms to test: advanced infrastructure, technology, large-scale production, effective logistics, high output per free household, and exclusion of enslaved populations from benefits. These are analytical possibilities, not newly approved implementations.

## Canopy Kingdoms — Idyllic fantasy prosperity for freefolk
- Freefolk live in a classic high-fantasy setting, with a more idyllic standard of living because of high social order.
- Automation and organized slavery both contribute to the economy.
- Free citizens can become wealthy comparatively easily, particularly when able and willing to exploit labor and productive resources.
- Important inequality: free citizens' prosperity does not imply the same for enslaved Bloomkin or other suppressed peoples.

## Scarlands — Extreme wealth concentration and precarious freedom
- The most brutal of the three societies, a dystopia where escaping enslavement or conditions equivalent to it requires strength, skill, connections, or guile.
- Wealth is extremely concentrated.
- Author analogizes its system to anarchocapitalism.
- **Canon-compatibility note:** Existing Vren Blood Courts and hierarchical contractual systems remain operative. Explore how competing private/coercive authorities, market power and weak universal protections coexist with those political structures rather than asserting literally no governing institutions.
- Vitae extraction from living people and dangerous alchemical industry belong in the regional production model; no unsupported extraction yields or currency rates.

## Canopy benchmark households (PROPOSED, not established NPCs)

### A. Middle-class free Thornguard outside a noble house
- A wage-earning or salaried working adult; possible occupation: skilled workshop supervisor or municipal official (both suggestions only).
- Analyze housing, staple food, varied diet, utilities/heating, clothing, healthcare, taxes, leisure, durable consumer goods and savings.
- Distinguish rental versus ownership, single adult versus dependents, and legal protections.

### B. More prosperous free Thornguard with enslaved Bloomkin
- A successful merchant or business proprietor, without noble title; domestic and/or commercial Bloomkin labor.
- Analyze business revenue and costs, household assets, enslaved people's work output and welfare, food/shelter/clothing/health provisions, supervision and coercion costs, financial risks, and access to leisure.
- Do not assume ownership by itself makes someone prosperous or confuse the owner's financial accounting with enslaved people's well-being.

## Cross-check against established racial lore
From main-codex `02_Races.md`: Thornguard are ratfolk with martial aristocratic Banners, not exclusively noble in the proposed economic example. Bloomkin are mousefolk and are primarily enslaved companions/craftspeople/Shieldmates. The historical Oath of Protection subordinated Bloomkin within hereditary service. Their magic depends on Concordance with Thornguard; do not describe Bloomkin as independently powering major white-mana machinery without approval.
