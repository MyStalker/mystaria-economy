# Canopy Household Budgets — DRAFT calibration

**Status:** User-established social setting and household specifications; **ALL numerical silver budgets and financial comparisons below are UNAPPROVED EXAMPLES**, not canon, market quotations, or exchanged weights of real silver.

## Latest author decisions, November 21, 2026
- Urban Canopy housing: renting is normal, inspired by Roman urban housing; homeownership more like Roman social and ownership patterns, qualifying the older preliminary suggestion that a modest home could universally be paid off within one year. The specific terms for owning a house are not yet fixed. Roman analogies are about patterns, not transplanted historical numbers.
- Household income and financial understanding: 1950s postwar US one-income model is helpful, with Roman-style household commerce and labor as supplementary context.
- Canopy freefolk leisure: frequent drinks, tournaments, recreational swords, possible firearms as hobbies, holidays. Roman-style shared bathhouses, fountains, civic spaces, festivals, markets and other amenities are also desired.
- Noble territorial controls and race matter independently of wealth: free Moontouched might face constant pressure toward 'protection' or adoption into noble households; villages tied to a noble house may allow a Moontouched to reside only as a guest or conditional dependent. Mixed cities have more fluid control, but independent life is still more viable for an already prosperous, useful or connected person. Do not invent a universal statute here.
- Comparative food cost: cheapest in technologically advanced Imperium, where **Worldspindles** enable pseudo-modern agriculture; middle in lush, productive but more traditionally agricultural Canopy Kingdoms; Scarlands **at least double** the equivalent reference food cost, with major consequences for household purchasing power. Exchange/cost baskets must be normalized before these claims become numeric local prices.
- Taxes can vary with the local dragon's preferences, e.g., patronage of art vs accumulation of gold. Local law, rate, administration and dragon-to-noble obligations OPEN.
- Household A: free non-noble **Thornguard town guard**, hired by local noble house, regular hours, returns home rather than assigned to barracks; enforces law and sometimes handles slavery-related issues; **five-person household**. One monetary income should be viable; possibility of supplementary spouse income remains OPEN.
- Household B: better-off Thornguard runs a local **dressmaking workshop/shop**, with **five enslaved Bloomkin**, in a reasonably urbanized town; sells ready-made garments in-house and accepts local commissions, not major long-distance export.
- Enslaved Bloomkin in this household retain relatively favorable hours (ordinary 40h week, longer seasonal peaks), sick time, maternity leave, some education, evenings, community life, holidays and casual personal clothing.
- **Ownership transfer:** Thornguard noble houses normally trade or gift enslaved people only to allies due to their duty of protection and honor codes; exceptions exist. The proprietor's five Bloomkin were **more likely inherited** than obtained via an agreement/loan under which the proprietor gradually joins the noble house. Market cash purchase is not to be assumed the normal route. Distinguish actual legal title, custodianship, obligations, debt, and exit restrictions when developed. This is the author's plausibility ranking, not a fully specified inheritance law.

## Household A: proposed illustrative annual budget (not canon)
Accounting only: 'silver units' are invented normalization markers, **not minted coin identities, metal weights or exchange rates**. Assume the five-person household has one monetary earner, 240 paid days per year, 12.5 units per paid day, salary 3,000 units per year, urban rental and extensive public amenities.

| Category | Units per year | Income share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Food and ordinary drink | 900 | 30% |
| Rent | 450 | 15% |
| Clothes and household goods | 225 | 7.5% |
| Heat, utilities and upkeep | 180 | 6% |
| Tax and civic obligations | 240 | 8% |
| Tournaments, taverns and hobbies | 300 | 10% |
| Healthcare, education and insurance | 300 | 10% |
| Savings and investments | 405 | 13.5% |
| **TOTAL** | **3000** | **100%** |

Caveats: this is a stress-test allocation, not verified production-equilibrium. Need adjust for what the employer pays, direct vs indirect tax, public healthcare, free bathhouses, food caloric requirements of large Thornguard, wages relative to skilled Bloomkin, local land control, and childcare. A single monetary wage does not mean the other adult has no unpaid domestic labor. Do not set firearm or sword prices from these accounting units without actual manufacturing analysis.

## Household B: modeling plan
Separate household budget from business income statement:
- Revenues by walk-in ready-made garment and local commissions.
- Fabric/trim inputs and tools, storefront space, equipment depreciation, utilities, public dues and local taxes.
- Five Bloomkin's food, housing, clothing, health, time off, education, family continuity and opportunity for community activities; any direct personal allowances.
- Proprietor's own labor, management time, risks and living costs.
- Inherited workers' opportunity cost and transfers from noble relations vs paid worker wage cost; compare return on capital, NOT simply maintenance vs salaries.
- A wage-paying comparator must represent available **skilled Bloomkin or other equivalent workers** and real discriminatory labor access, not arbitrarily assign the town guard's salary to them.
- Provisioning and welfare are not substitutes for legal freedom or ability to refuse work.
