# Mystaria: Economic Principles and Labor

## Author-established design direction (November 21, 2026)
- The setting has extraordinarily widespread slavery. Among enslaved races, free people are a minority. Most adventurers are likely to come from slave-master races or races socially adjacent to them. Precise regional demographic shares, occupational splits, and ownership practices have not yet been numerically defined.
- The economy must not be modeled as a typical society of predominantly free wage earners.
- Independent, free workers remain an important **benchmark**, including a free Kharak with a regular working schedule who pays for necessities and extras.
- A separate comparison is the resource burden and incentives of a slaveholder maintaining five Kharak, including food, clothing, and discretionary provisions used to incentivize compliance.
- Avoid flattening these into an identical economic model: involuntary extraction and violence affect autonomy, health, productivity, incentives, and allocation of gains.
- Fantasy technology, divine systems, and the existing races and crafts can produce large regional differences; avoid presuming medieval European or contemporary labor conditions everywhere.

## Recommended analytical distinction (PROPOSED methodology, not an approved rule)
**Free-worker household ledger**
- Gross wages, other earned income, seasonal labor, housing, food, heating, clothing, medicine, transport, taxes, dependents, savings and discretionary consumption.
- Actual time worked, skill, bargaining power, local opportunities and living standard.

**Enslaved-labor production ledger**
- Work output and sale value; rations, shelter, clothing, health, supervision, forced labor management, loss of skilled capability, captivity/transport, risk of injury/death, and property/ownership arrangements.
- A slaveholder's accounting does not measure the person's welfare or true social cost; account for cruelty, coercion, and suppressed alternatives rather than implicitly treating forced labor as freely chosen employment.
- Distinguish real output per working day, maintenance spending, market exchange or sale-price speculation, and the share appropriated by an owner.

**Regional equilibrium**
- Baseline productive capacity and subsistence demand, geography, climate, logistics, trade barriers, ownership institutions and magical energy/Hex pollution.
- Prices may differ from production cost because of scarcity, monopoly, taxes, financing, violence and breakdown of transport.

## First reference case (OPEN)
An independent Kharak household with a regular wage-paying occupation. Before selecting numbers, establish household composition, town/country context, working time, common food basket, housing norms, coin denomination, and ownership structures in the chosen region. Compare to a five-Kharak enslaved labor unit separately.

No numerical wage, budget, living cost, slave valuation, output ratio or regional population share has been approved.
