At 8 h/day conventional worker equivalents: 7,300/8 = **912.5 conventional worker-days per year**. With Leshy performing exactly the 2,000 h pasture-establishment tasks at 5×, actual 400 h /8 = **50 Leshy specialist-days**, plus **5,300 ordinary-equivalent h = 662.5 other worker-days**, for 712.5 worker-days total; **21.9% fewer hands-on workdays**. At 250 working days/year this is ~2.85 full-time-equivalent employees spread across seasons; does NOT replace substantial peak-harvest crews, irrigation, night guarding or livestock labor.

**Compensation comparison, not currency:** Let one ordinary eight-hour paid workday = **1 LABOR-VALUE UNIT (LVU)** as a relative economic accounting numeraire, not a coin or currency. If specialist wage is **p LVU per Leshy day**, direct wage expenditure is **662.5+50p LVU** versus **912.5 LVU** entirely conventional. At p=3 -> **812.5 LVU**, saving 100 LVU or **10.96% of conventional wage bill**; at p=5 break-even; >5 costs more in direct wages before improved crop survival/quality is valued. This is not the purchase cost of an enslaved Leshy, who might receive zero cash wages but still has nonzero capital, living, support and security costs. No enslaved labor is automatically socially/morally costless, and no general rule sets free Leshy pay at 3×.

Direct-wage portion per tonne under p=3: 812.5/1,200=**0.6771 LVU per tonne** (~0.000307 LVU per dry pound). This is NOT a selling price and excludes overhead, land, water, machines, capital, transport, field damage, and business profits.

### Water source versus irrigation distribution
A conceptual **4mm/day** crop evapotranspiration or delivered-water-equivalent on 100ha is **4,000,000 liters/day total** from ALL sources. Rainfall, rootwater and stored water must be subtracted before computing irrigation deficit. If the *actual deficit* under a dry-period scenario is 4m L/day, design a reference full aqueduct of **4m L/day capacity**; then a complete Wakecraft Tidekin workforce, under user's approved comparison, supplies **2m L/day useful delivered irrigation equivalent**. Therefore 2 complete labor forces or 1 workforce + half-sized aqueduct could replace one full aqueduct at that particular crop deficit, *if a physical water source and feasible Wakecraft range/mana budgets exist*. This 4m/day aqueduct is farm-specific illustrative sizing, not a universal aqueduct metric. If rain supplies half of the hypothetical daily demand, one full Tidekin workforce can meet the remaining deficit; do not attribute rain to Tidekin or magic.

Need explicitly determine Tidekin WORKFORCE headcount, caster ranks, scheduling, water movement/lifting limits, hours/day, water source and mana recovery before any absolute workforce/wage/captive acquisition cost or market price.

### Approach for costing a genuine production price
COGS per tonne = (annual ordinary wages + specialist pay + annualized acquisition and upkeep of enslaved personnel + water rights/pumping/transport + aqueduct depreciation/maintenance OR Tidekin irrigation service/care + harvest machine ownership+fuel/repair + buildings+storage + crop losses + administration + land use/opportunity cost)/1,200 tonnes; final market selling price adds profit margin/financing/market risk and freight. A service performed by a captive has no direct wage but carries real costs. The founder's unpaid personal labor also has opportunity cost.
