# Canopy Workforce, Labor Allocation and Living-Treetop Settlements — DRAFT

**Status:** November 21, 2026 experimental workforce study. Race anatomy and treetop urban landscape are author-defined and reflected in the owning [main codex 38](https://github.com/MyStalker/mystaria-codex/blob/main/38_Leshen_Leshy_Foundations.md). All workforce counts, participation rates, output and estate distributions here are **PROPOSALS, not approved canon**.

## Working region and demographics

From [06 Food Demand](06_Canopy_Settlement_and_Food_Demand_DRAFT.md) and [07 Farm Footprint](07_Canopy_Farm_Footprint_DRAFT.md): 4,000-person market town; 6,000 rural residents; ~30–40 km² land primarily used for agriculture (highly provisional); 80–150 km² mixed district. The town's 75% smaller/default enslaved / 10% Centaur / 10% Thornguard / 5% Leshy is author-selected for economic experiment. The rural distribution of 4,200 smaller enslaved, 300 Centaur, 600 Thornguard, 900 Leshy is **assistant proposal only**, not authorized for broader geography.

- The great tree canopies sustain urban settlements, hanging gardens and elevated buildings, alongside surface roads, farms, rivers and markets.
- Urban Leshy use long body vines for mobility and leverage, tending living trees, decorative gardens, branch orchards, hanging crops and domestic vegetation. **Do not automatically assign town's 200 Leshy to commercial agriculture**; the 900 proposed countryside Leshy includes nonworkers and nonfarm workers.
- Leshy need light, water and soil; vines do not give free mass or magic, unbounded Strength, or unrestricted flight.
- Because noble houses control large enslaved populations and territory, workers can be relocated among holdings, but practical costs and limits remain: training, housing, family/network disruption, transport, seasonality, legal/customary noble obligations and productivity. Neither universally costless mobility nor universal centralized allocation is canon.

## Experimental rural workforce census (conservative mixed-labor scenario)
**Rural residents:** 6,000. For bookkeeping only, model 3,900 people engaged in regular paid, forced, household or seasonal productive activity; 2,100 dependent/nonregular workers (age, incapacity, etc). These 3,900 are **not 3,900 full-time equivalent wage workers**. No empirical demographic age distribution yet.

| Function | Worker headcount proposed |
|---|---:|
| Field crops | 420 |
| Orchards, vegetables, specialty horticulture | 380 |
| Livestock | 240 |
| Food storage, preparation and processing | 160 |
| Forestry and tree care | 250 |
| Clockwork operation, repair and infrastructure | 170 |
| Transport and hauling | 160 |
| Other crafts and manufacture | 420 |
| Domestic labor, care, family production | 850 |
| Trade and services | 360 |
| Civic functions, supervision and security | 150 |
| Other/seasonal/mixed work | 340 |
| **TOTAL** | **3,900** |

Primary food-production work: 420+380+240=**1,040 heads** = 17.33% rural residents, 10.4% whole region. Food processing adds 160. **IMPORTANT:** Many of these may not be full-time field equivalents because of seasonal assignments; do not treat this as validated advanced-clockwork productivity.

At previous illustrative 1,529 ha active cropland, field 420 heads share 627 grain ha plus 376 feed crop ha (~1,003 ha = 2.39 ha/head), and horticulture 380 heads share 526 ha (~1.38 ha/head); 240 livestock heads cover provisional 1,000–2,000 ha pasture (~4.17–8.33 ha/head), with intensive husbandry work excluded. This low crop-area-per-head allocation is labor-intensive; it may overstaff production if Leshy Craft and clockwork meaningfully improve labor efficiency. This is why an advanced-efficiency variant should be tested separately.

## Advanced clockwork/Leshy labor-efficiency variant (PROPOSAL)
Hold agriculture land and harvest targets constant, while replacing permanent full-season labor intensity with **120 full-year field-crop workers, 140 horticulture workers, and 80 animal-husbandry workers = 340 direct food-production equivalents**. Field worker maintains ~8.36 ha cropped grain/feed, horticulture worker ~3.76 ha mixed orchards/vegetables, animal worker ~12.5–25 ha pasture. **These are mathematical scenarios, NOT proof approved Mystaria machinery/magic can achieve the required throughput.**

Relative to 1,040 conservative heads, this hypothetically frees **700 equivalent permanent roles** for crafts, transport, civic infrastructure, care, manufacturing, other estate work, leisure or Scarlands-bound exports, but the actual released workers and their rights depend on noble choices and the labor market. Mechanization can also displace enslaved workers without granting them freedom.

**Critical check:** Gross field labor demands peak sharply at sowing and harvest. Seasonal movements of bound workers and temporary crews from other occupations are plausible with noble estates, but training bottlenecks, machinery maintenance and opportunistic allocation should be explicitly modeled. Record permanent workforce and seasonal peak separately; no authority has approved arbitrary redeployment with zero costs.

## Proposed split of rural Leshy roles (900 people; scenario ONLY)
Do not equate population with workers. Example:
- 250 participate in food-crop or orchard horticulture.
- 100 participate in forestry, canopy-tree care, conservation or soil restoration.
- 100 participate in nursery, greenhouse, rare plant and botanical commerce.
- 100 participate in households, community production, markets and education.
- 350 are children, elderly, otherwise nonworking or engaged in other occupations.
These categories include part-time/seasonal work and do NOT imply every Leshy belongs to the nobility. Class and freedom not yet defined.

**Town Leshy (200)**: proposed example roles 60 tree infrastructure/hanging gardens; 40 household ornamental horticulture; 30 specialty nurseries/medicine; 30 commerce/services; 40 children/other. This is not approved and should not be used to pad food production.

## Enslavement and labor mobility
Most of the smaller residents in the experimental town and rural census are enslaved; formal freedom distributions of Centaurs, Thornguard and Leshy remain to be defined. Labor mobility is predominantly **owner-directed** between holdings, not automatically freely selected by workers; retain households/family effects, legal agreements, tenancy, religious duties and social resistance. Crop output cannot be assigned all to masters as though they bear zero housing/food/health/support expenses. Agricultural provisioning includes slave maintenance as part of the economic flow.
