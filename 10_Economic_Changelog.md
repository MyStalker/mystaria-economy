# Mystaria Economy: Decision Changelog

## 2026-11-21 — Foundation: Author-described currency, slavery, and non-Hero goals

**Recorded author directions:**
- Three major national currencies plus two minor currencies (Serpentari precious-metal coinage among the minors; second minor undecided).
- Imperium **Scales**: sophisticated paper currency associated with gold-standard banking.
- Canopy Kingdoms: silver primary, gold secondary.
- Scarlands **Vitae**: condensable, industrially useful liquid essence used in Pazara houses and as basis of potion craft. Measured in ounces and colors: **red standard, white middle, pure clear rarest**. **Marks rejected** as a currency name.
- Real Vitae is extracted from living people by alchemists through torturous methods; its supply is affected by extraction and consumption/transformation. Counterfeiters instead make cheaper convincing false Vitae.
- Divine Gems and Glints are exclusive to gods' divine shops, unknown to ordinary non-Heroes without special expertise. Existing Gem tariffs and reward progression preserved.
- Independent non-Hero player progression is an intended experience; an unrelated centaur smith can make QV swords without divine Gems.
- Slavery is exceptionally widespread. Free members of commonly enslaved races are a minority; model free Kharak wages/expenses as a benchmark and enslaved Kharak maintenance/production as a distinct comparison, not a universal wage market.

**Not yet canonized as quantitative rules:** wage schedules, cost-of-living baskets, ounces-to-colors values, Scarlands Vitae extraction yields, silver/gold weights, Scales redemption, Gem-mortal conversion, exact slavery demographics, second minor currency, non-Hero XP/progression formulas.

**Integration:** Cross-reference [mystaria-codex](https://github.com/MyStalker/mystaria-codex) as mechanical canon and resolve any conflict before claiming new numbers are approved.

## November 21, 2026 — AUTHOR-APPROVED MOSSBACK BLOOM AND COMMUNAL REARING

**Latest explicit author approval, superseding prior speculative brood counts:** The preferred typical Mossback Bloom produces **about 30 seeds per mother**, each initially **ostrich-egg-sized**. **A handful of mothers normally enter Bloom together**, forming communal protected breeding congregations; no fixed number of simultaneous mothers or percentage of females breeding has been approved. Pollination is the previously stated preferred reproductive mechanism.

During **the Bloom**, a mother becomes stationary and hibernation-like for **approximately one season**, essentially defenseless and sacrificing a great deal of her bodily biomass. Seeds **hatch/develop over approximately one month**, nourished by maternal milk while they are seeds. Some excess milk can be harvested, and **milk theft** is a real risk to defenseless mothers. Once the sprouts become ambulatory they can **begin incorporating normal feed**; other herd females can **supplement their milk**, enabling the birth mother to rest and recover. By the time sprouts depart, she is about **half her former size**. Earlier explicit description included half **height** and half **mass**; retain both as working biological intent without forcing geometrically proportional shrinkage or a conservation formula. Full physical recovery requires an extended period, exact length not newly fixed here.

**Approved assistant recommendations:** Mossbacks have a **strong communal protection instinct toward Blooming females, including unrelated mothers**. Mixed-age, mixed-reproductive-state herds create economic advantages in nursery defense and wet-nursing. Milk is both a tradeable renewable product and a critical rearing input, so sale/theft can compete with offspring survival. Specialized **breeding sanctuaries, sunlit plains nurseries and forest finishing/slaughter ranches** are a sound organizational model, not an edict that every herd uses all three. Defenseless Bloomers create security risks, and sunlight, feed, water and minerals must be counted in mass-growth economics; maternal mass loss alone is not assumed to literally supply all juvenile adult biomass.

**Still OPEN:** exact lactation volumes, brood variability around 30, pollination triggers, time to walking/independence, age at maturity, juvenile survival, maternal recovery years, pollination interval, spore function during Bloom, actual energy allocation, and ranch profitability. **Older scenario math using eight seeds or elephant-style single calves is superseded as a Mossback baseline and retained only as historical what-if math.**


## November 21, 2026 — AUTHOR DIRECTION: MOSSBACK JUVENILE MORTALITY, CARE AND MATURATION

**Latest author's selected developmental ages:** Mossbacks reach **pollination/reproductive maturity around age 12 years**, and **full adult physical size around age 20 years**. Those are distinct thresholds: reproductive eligibility at 12 does not mean full body mass, maximum production, or automatic practical breeding success. Maintain the prior approved **about 30 ostrich-egg-sized seeds per Bloom**, multiple communal Blooming mothers, seed milk dependence and shared nursing.

**Ecological direction:** Juvenile/seed survival should be **relatively low**, helping explain large broods, but **NO survival percentage has been approved**. Causes of attrition include unequal access to milk (an overconsuming seed can starve smaller runts), pests and parasites, small predators, disease, **even trace Hex affecting developing seeds**, and a sick/dying Blooming mother potentially losing her entire brood. Specific Hex thresholds, disease outcomes and mortality stages are not quantified; do not treat trace exposure as a universal automatic kill mechanic beyond this species' ecological vulnerability.

**Care and husbandry:** Mossback nurseries have a **high need for Nymbrin healers practiced in living alchemy**, alchemical medical remedies, and **Leshy who cultivate the growing young**. Existing Nymbrin Vital Transmutation permits tissue repair and biological intervention under normal access, Power, material and Hex rules; this direction does not create free or Hexless healing. **NEW PROPOSAL, NOT APPROVED:** separate alchemical treatment/waste from vulnerable seed beds to reduce exposure, subject to how Hex transport is ultimately modeled. **NEW ILLUSTRATIVE ECONOMIC TEST ONLY:** evaluate survival bands of 10–30% through full size, without treating 20% or any other value as canon. Survival to age 12 and age 20 may differ significantly.

**Economics:** A 30-seed cohort's usable output is delayed by up to 20 years for full-size livestock, with earlier potentially fertile 12-year animals. Production planning must include juvenile feeding, nursing competition, disease control, alchemical healing costs/Hex disposal, Leshy support, maternal single-point brood failure, mortality and space. Reproductive interval, maternal regeneration and maturity survival remain OPEN. Do not project a guaranteed adult yield or approved numerical milk price.


## November 21, 2026 — AUTHOR-SELECTED MOSSBACK SURVIVAL BENCHMARKS

**Approved planning baseline:** Of the roughly **30 seeds per Bloom**, an estimated **25% survive to full maturity in the wild**, compared with **75% under competent captivity/rearing**. These are typical comparative ecological expectations, not guaranteed outcomes for an individual brood or a mandatory mechanical dice rule. On a per-Bloom expected-value basis, that is **7.5 fully mature wild survivors** versus **22.5 captive survivors** (fractional numbers denote cohort averages, not individual animals). In a four-mother, 120-seed communal Bloom, corresponding expected eventual full-grown survivors are **30 wild** versus **90 captive**. The 75% figure presumes substantial animal husbandry, communal nursing, Leshy tending, disease/pest control, Nymbrin veterinary interventions and appropriate protection against Hex; it is not survival without associated costs.

Mossbacks reach **reproductive maturity at approximately 12 years**, **full physical maturity around 20 years**. These survival percentages are taken here as survival **from seed to full-size age 20**, for comparison with the existing economic model; survival to reproductive age 12 is **not separately quantified**, nor are age-specific death rates. Relative to the previous unapproved exploratory 10–30% band, the 25% wild and 75% captive baselines take precedence. Bloom frequency, maternal recovery, herd composition, veterinary costs and total saleable yield remain OPEN. Do not treat the captive advantage as a free multiplier on net profitability.


## November 21, 2026 — AUTHOR-APPROVED MOSSBACK SIX-YEAR POST-BLOOM RECOVERY AND STAGGERED SEASONS

**Latest explicit author decision:** Following a Mossback mother's Bloom, she regains **most of her fighting/defensive strength in approximately two years**. At this same **two-year** milestone, her milk production returns to **normal amounts** (ordinary baseline quantities remain unquantified). However, restoring enough fat and other bodily reserves for **another Bloom takes the full six years of recovery**. This is a **six-year post-Bloom recovery** benchmark, not merely a vague interval between births; a new Bloom is not assumed before adequate recovery. This supersedes earlier unspecified multi-year recovery guesses, without changing the approved approximately 30 seeds/Bloom or Bloom's approximately one-season hibernation.

**Seasonal group staggering:** Mossback herds **take turns Blooming in seasonal cohorts**, so only a handful of mothers normally enter Bloom together while others can defend the herd, share milk and care for offspring. It is not established that all females in a herd Bloom in one season or that every calendar season has a cohort.
